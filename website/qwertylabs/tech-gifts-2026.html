tech giftsfor kids, 2026

Gifts chosen for you

Because who has time?

From tiny inventions and quirky stocking fillers to programmable robots and one completely unreasonable Christmas wish. Chosen by the people who spend their days making things with children.

Australian prices checked 28 November 2026. Postage extra.

OUR PICKS

01 · Kikkerland Dog Cable Ties

Even charging cables deserve a pet. Three little dogs keep the tangles in check and make a desk look considerably more cheerful. Quite possibly the world's cutest cable management strategy.

Stocking filler · no programming required

$14.95 · Find it at Wileaway

02 · JAKI Mini Arcade Brick Kit

Build a tiny arcade cabinet with 210 bricks, then admire your astonishingly small gaming empire. It's a display model, not a working video game. Which is probably good news for everyone's screen-time negotiations.

210 pieces · a model to build, not a console

$18.95 · Find it at SuperOffice

03 · Makedo EXPLORE

All those delivery boxes finally have a purpose. Safe saws and reusable screws turn cardboard into castles, vending machines and suspiciously ambitious robots. Excellent for making something wonderfully enormous.

50 pieces · ages 5+

$28.00 · Find it at CD-Soft

04 · BBC micro:bit V2 GO Bundle

Lights, buttons, sensors and sound in a pocket-sized computer. Make a reaction game, a step counter or an alarm to detect anyone invading your bedroom. Still one of our favourite ways to start making with code.

Board, batteries and USB cable included

$30.55 · Find it at Little Bird Electronics

05 · Raspberry Pi Pico W Electronics Kit

A little board and a box of actual electronic bits: LEDs, switches, resistors and a breadboard. Build things that light up, beep or respond to the world. A brilliant next step for the aspiring gadget engineer.

Photo shows the Pico W board; the full kit includes components

$37.22 · Find it at Little Bird Electronics

06 · Edison Robot V3

A clever little robot that follows lines, senses obstacles and plays nicely with LEGO-compatible bricks. The rechargeable V3 is proof that robotics doesn't need a robot-dog-sized budget.

Rechargeable · LEGO-compatible

$57.49 · Find it at Edtechs

07 · Kitronik ARCADE

First make the game. Then play it on a real handheld. Characters, levels, power-ups and improbable difficulty settings are all up to the child. Suddenly 'I played video games all day' sounds rather productive.

Programmable handheld · USB cable and AA batteries extra

$79.60 · Find it at Core Electronics

08 · Turing Tumble

Build a computer powered by falling marbles. No screen, no batteries, just satisfyingly clattery logic puzzles and the remarkable discovery that a machine can calculate without a chip in sight.

Mechanical logic · ages 8 to adult

$124.42 · Find it at Upper Story Australia

09 · Codey Rocky

Codey has an expressive face. Rocky has wheels. Together they're a programmable character that can follow lines, recognise colours and respond to a surprisingly rich assortment of sensors. A longtime favourite in our orbit.

Blocks to Python · confirm store stock before ordering

$159.99 · Find it at Metro Hobbies

THE RIDICULOUS

10 · NEO, the robot butler

Meet the life-sized humanoid being developed to fetch things, help with chores and converse like a rather expensive housemate. Some jobs still need a human operator behind the scenes, so maybe don't retire the dishwasher just yet.

A real US preorder, not a fully autonomous household servant. Our deliberately international, completely outrageous exception.

US$20,000 · Meet NEO at 1X
