teensmake something bigger

Make bigger things in our weekly classes and holiday camps.

AGES 13–17

More independence. More say in what you make.

Games, AI, electronics, robotics and bigger ideas. Start something new or bring a project of your own. You don’t need to know how to code to get started.

Who?

Teenagers aged 13–17, from complete beginners to makers who've been coding for years.

Some are keen to design their own games or websites; others would rather be building robots, wiring up electronics or experimenting with AI.

Some started with us as kids. Others join as teenagers. You don't need any previous coding experience.

What?

Games, robots, AI experiments, electronics, websites, digital worlds and things that respond to light, sound or movement.

Design a game with your own mechanics, build a connected gadget, program a robot or turn an idea into a working prototype.

We use Scratch, Python, JavaScript, Raspberry Pi, microcontrollers, sensors and plenty of things with wires. We choose the tools to suit the project, not the other way around.

As you gain experience, there's room to take on bigger projects, explore unfamiliar technology and decide what you want to make next.

At 14, Zach and Chloe built Project Code Cloud, an interactive electronics project that became a VIP judge’s favourite at the global Coolest Projects 2026 showcase. Not bad for a project that started with a question.

Why?

Making gives learning a reason.

It's one thing to follow instructions and get something working. It's much more interesting to understand it, change it, break it, fix it and make it your own.

A project gives you a reason to learn coding, logic, electronics and design. Want two devices to talk to each other? Now you've got a good reason to explore how data moves between them.

We like hearing, “What happens if I try this?” Particularly when nobody knows the answer yet.

How?

We teach in small groups, with individual guidance and room to work independently.

Beginners start with guided projects. As you gain experience, we help you plan, prototype and develop ideas of your own. Sometimes the group starts together. Other days, everybody is making something different.

With around one tutor for seven students, there's someone to ask when things don't work. We check in regularly so nobody spends weeks stuck on the same problem.

We've been teaching creative technology since 2016. Our tutors write code and make things with technology too.

There's a fair bit of talking, laughing and trying out each other's creations. Zach wrote about being a student here when he was 13. He reckons we're funny. Well, most of the time.

If your teen has particular support needs or finds busy environments difficult, get in touch and we can talk through which group might suit them.

“Being a student here for over three years has encouraged me to take the skills I learned in these classes into my everyday life.”

Zach · Student, writing at 13

Where?

Upstairs in Marrickville, Sydney's Inner West.

Level 1, 230 Marrickville Road, Marrickville NSW 2204.

Look for the entrance next to Pizza Hut, near Victoria Road. We've put together some tips for getting here.

Every student has their own computer. We provide all the equipment, so there's no need to bring a laptop.

“At QwertyKids, you’ll never feel left out even if you’re not one to talk.”

Zach · Student, writing at 13

When?

Weekly classes

75 minutes weekly, usually nine sessions per term. Start a project, return to an idea over several weeks or get the tricky bits working. New students can join during a term when there's a suitable place.

School holiday camps

A whole day to try something new or take an idea further. Make a game, program a robot or experiment with electronics. Different projects run on different days. Come for one or several. Teens can join general camp days or ask us about Teen Cadets, our dedicated group for teenagers.
