OUR COMMITMENT

Making things should be adventurous. Feeling safe shouldn't be a question.

Qwerty Labs, formerly QwertyKids ("Qwerty"), is a creative technology education studio in Marrickville, Sydney. We work with children and teenagers through weekly classes, holiday camps, workshops and mentoring.

Every child has the right to feel safe, respected, included and heard. We do not tolerate child abuse, grooming, bullying, harassment, discrimination or other harmful behaviour. This policy applies to our in-person and online activities and to staff, contractors, volunteers, teen helpers and visitors involved in those activities.

Listening to children

Children are welcome to ask questions, make suggestions, say no to inappropriate contact and tell us when something is wrong. We take concerns seriously, including concerns about the conduct of an adult or another child. Children can speak to a Qwerty teacher, a trusted adult or an outside support service. They do not need permission to raise a safety concern.

We aim to support children of different cultures, backgrounds, identities and abilities. We pay particular attention to the safety of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children, children with disability, and children who may face additional barriers to speaking up. We listen to children about what helps them feel safe and participate.

Our people and their responsibilities

Adults working in child-related roles must meet applicable NSW Working with Children Check requirements before undertaking those roles. Qwerty verifies required clearances through the NSW Office of the Children's Guardian, rather than relying only on a card or certificate. We use role-appropriate recruitment, references, induction and supervision.

Helpers under 18 are not substitutes for adult teachers or supervisors. They are guided and supervised by responsible adults and expected to follow age-appropriate conduct rules.

Every adult and helper must follow our Child Safety Code of Conduct. Screening is only one part of keeping children safe; we also set clear boundaries, respond to concerns and review our practices.

Safe teaching and supervision

We plan activities with regard to each student's age, experience, accessibility and support needs. We assess foreseeable risks associated with electronics, tools, physical computing, online platforms and the studio environment, and provide supervision and instruction suited to the activity.

Staff avoid unnecessary one-to-one situations out of sight or hearing of others. Individual guidance happens in observable areas wherever practicable. Personal care and physical contact are handled respectfully, with the child's comfort and dignity in mind, and only as appropriate for safety or support.

Parents and carers should provide relevant emergency contacts and information needed for safe participation. We use that information discreetly, share it on a need-to-know basis and work with families to consider reasonable adjustments.

Online safety and communication

Online safety is part of creative technology education. We select learning tools with regard to age requirements, privacy and the risks of public accounts, messaging and user-generated content. We teach young people not to publish addresses, passwords or other identifying information and to ask for help with inappropriate material or contact.

Staff and helpers must not initiate private personal conversations with students through social media, gaming platforms, private messaging or personal accounts. Necessary program communications use approved Qwerty channels and include a parent or carer, or another appropriate Qwerty adult, where required.

We do not upload children's identifiable information, images, health details or private work into AI services or public platforms without an appropriate purpose, safeguards and permission.

Photos, videos and projects

We ask for appropriate parent or carer permission before publishing identifiable photos or videos of children or linking a child's name to publicly shared work. We also respect a young person's own wishes about being photographed or featured. Attendance does not amount to publicity consent.

Families can request that we stop future use of an image and remove material from channels Qwerty controls, subject to applicable legal requirements and technical limits. We cannot guarantee removal of copies made by others.

RAISE A CONCERN

Anyone — including a child — can raise a concern, whether it seems small or serious. Speak to a teacher or contact Dannielle Chun, Head Teacher, using the details below. If the concern is about the Head Teacher, or you do not wish to raise it with Qwerty, contact the relevant external authority directly.

We listen without judgement, take immediate safety steps where needed, record relevant information securely, and make reports to police, child protection authorities or other regulators when required or appropriate. We do not ask children to investigate or repeatedly retell allegations. Reporting a concern in good faith will not lead to retaliation.

  • Immediate danger: call 000.
  • Suspected abuse or neglect: NSW Child Protection Helpline 13 21 11 (24 hours).
  • Support for children and young people: Kids Helpline 1800 55 1800 (24 hours).

Qwerty's internal Child Safety Reporting Procedure guides staff and helpers in responding, recording, escalating and preserving information. It does not prevent anyone from making a direct report to an authority.

Reviewing our approach

We review child safety practices as risks, programs and legal requirements change, and seek feedback from children, families and staff. Our approach is guided by the NSW Child Safe Standards and current Australian online safety guidance.

OUR CODE

For teachers, contractors, volunteers, teen helpers and others working with children at Qwerty

Young people come to Qwerty Labs to make things, ask questions and follow their ideas. Our job is to give them the freedom to do that in a safe, respectful environment.

This Code applies in the studio, at events, online and whenever someone is representing Qwerty in an activity involving children. Staff and helpers must understand it before beginning their role. Adults are responsible for maintaining professional boundaries; children are never responsible for managing an adult's behaviour.

Always

  • Put children's safety and dignity ahead of convenience, publicity or project outcomes.
  • Treat each child fairly and respectfully, including children with disability, First Nations children and children from different backgrounds and identities.
  • Use encouragement, patience and constructive feedback. Make room for children's opinions, questions and decisions.
  • Follow the agreed supervision arrangements, know where participants are, and keep individual instruction observable wherever practicable.
  • Explain activities and safety rules at a level the child can understand. Check suitability before using tools, electronics, external websites or AI services.
  • Respect personal space and ask before non-essential physical contact. Any necessary contact must be appropriate, proportionate and for safety or support, with the child's comfort and dignity considered.
  • Use approved Qwerty systems for communications, photographs, records and student accounts.
  • Include a parent/carer or another appropriate Qwerty adult in necessary student communications outside class.
  • Report any safeguarding concern immediately in accordance with the reporting procedure, even if the concern seems minor or involves a colleague.
  • Keep information about children confidential except where sharing is needed for safety, proper supervision or lawful reporting.

Never

  • Bully, shame, threaten, humiliate, discriminate against, groom or sexually harass a child.
  • Use corporal punishment or physically restrain a child except where immediately necessary and lawful to prevent serious harm.
  • Seek an inappropriate personal or romantic relationship with a student, give special secret gifts, encourage secrecy or show favouritism that creates dependency.
  • Arrange unnecessary isolated one-to-one meetings, private transport or meetings at a private home with a student. Any unavoidable exception requires prior authorisation and appropriate safeguards.
  • Contact a child through personal social media, private messaging, gaming accounts or personal email for non-program purposes.
  • Share passwords, access private accounts without a proper reason, or ask students to disclose personal or family information unnecessarily.
  • Publish a child's identity, photographs, video, location or work linked to their identity without appropriate authorisation and consideration of the child's wishes.
  • Upload a child's sensitive details or identifiable photographs to public tools or AI services without an approved purpose and safeguards.
  • Ignore an unsafe situation or promise to keep a disclosure of abuse secret.
  • Investigate a child protection allegation yourself, confront the alleged offender, or coach a child's account.

One-to-one teaching and online sessions

Individual help is often an important part of Qwerty's teaching. Provide it within sight or easy awareness of others wherever practicable. If privacy is necessary for a sensitive conversation, let another responsible adult know where you are and follow a safe arrangement. For online mentoring, use approved channels and arrangements appropriate to the student's age; do not record or privately message students without authorisation.

Responding when a child tells you something

  1. Listen calmly. Take the child seriously. Thank them for telling you.
  2. Keep questions minimal. Ask only what is necessary to understand immediate safety. Do not lead or repeatedly question the child.
  3. Do not promise secrecy. Explain that you may need to tell someone who can help keep them safe.
  4. Act on immediate danger. Call 000 if urgent police or ambulance assistance is needed.
  5. Record and report promptly. Write down the child's words as accurately as possible, with time, date and your observations. Pass the information securely to the Head Teacher, or report directly to authorities when appropriate or if the concern involves the Head Teacher.

Child protection reporting duties apply according to the person's role and the nature of the concern. No Qwerty internal process prevents a person from reporting directly to police or child protection authorities.

Teen helpers

Teen helpers may assist with demonstrations, welcoming participants and encouraging projects under adult supervision. They must not be assigned sole responsibility for a group, handle serious disclosures alone or use private messaging with younger participants. They should tell the supervising adult promptly if they are worried about anyone's safety, including their own.

Breaches and concerns

A breach may result in additional guidance, changed duties, suspension, removal from child-related activities or referral to authorities, depending on the circumstances. We take concerns about adult behaviour seriously and do not retaliate against anyone who raises a concern in good faith.
