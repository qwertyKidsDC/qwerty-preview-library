AGES 7–12

Nine weekly sessions, small groups and time to get properly into a project.

Children aged 7–12 make games, program robots and experiment with electronics, picking up coding with individual guidance along the way.

Who?

Children aged 7–12, from complete beginners to experienced young makers.

Some arrive having never written a line of code. Others have built games at home, played with electronics or taught themselves a thing or two.

We place children in small groups by age and experience, so everyone has projects and guidance that suit them. Some sessions have separate groups running at the same time.

Teens aged 13–17 may join some of the same sessions, with their own projects and guidance. See weekly classes for teens.

What?

Games, robots, websites, electronics, AI experiments, digital worlds and things that connect code to the physical world.

A child might build a game with scoring, timers and levels, program a robot to respond to its surroundings, make something with a micro:bit or Raspberry Pi, or design and publish a website.

We use Scratch, Python, JavaScript, HTML, game engines, microcontrollers and whatever makes sense for the project and the student's experience.

Some projects take a session. Others grow over several weeks. There's always something else to try.

Why?

Making gives learning a reason.

A game needs rules. A robot needs instructions. A circuit needs to do something useful. Or at least something interesting.

Children learn coding, logic, design and problem-solving because they need those skills to make something work.

Weekly classes give them time to revisit ideas, experiment, work through tricky problems and gradually make more of their own decisions.

How?

Small groups, practical help and more independence as children find their feet.

Beginners start with guided projects. As they gain experience, children combine ideas, modify things they've made and develop projects of their own. These aren't fixed levels or a compulsory progression.

We work alongside them, explaining unfamiliar concepts, demonstrating techniques and helping when things get tricky. With around one tutor for seven children, there's room to try things independently and someone to ask when they get stuck.

It's an active studio, not a silent one. If your child has particular support needs or finds busy environments difficult, tell us when enrolling. We can talk through which group might suit them.

Where?

Upstairs in Marrickville, Sydney's Inner West.

Level 1, 230 Marrickville Road, next to Pizza Hut, near Victoria Road. Our studio is full of computers, robots, electronics and projects in various stages of becoming something.

Every child has their own computer. We provide all the equipment, so there's no need to bring a laptop.

When?

Weekly during school term

Each class runs for 75 minutes. Current timetable: Monday 4:45–6:00 pm; Tuesday 5:15–6:30 pm; Saturday 9:00–10:15 am, 10:30–11:45 am and 12:15–1:30 pm. Places depend on the age group and session.

Fees and enrolment

$270 for a nine-week term. New students can join during a term when there's a suitable place. We'll help find the right group.
