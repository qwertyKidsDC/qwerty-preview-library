Peterson language design

Rocky isn't just singing one melody.

The production system uses three principal simultaneous vocal lines, each drawing from a restricted seven-note set. Two lines mainly carry lexical information; a third helps mark word and grammatical structure.

Relative pitch matters

The Petersons designed it so humans would not necessarily need perfect pitch. Interval movement and relationships between notes can carry the useful pattern.

Delivery can add tone

Staccato, tremolo, pitch distortion and extra vocal channels were proposed as ways to add precision, hesitation, emotion or other pragmatic information.

Your advantage

Listen for interval shape, layered voices, rhythm, segmentation and recurrence before worrying about naming every absolute pitch.

The thing we still need to establish

Did the Peterson patterns survive into the finished Rocky?

The finished movie's sound team says Rocky uses about 250 words and concepts. Their assistant maintained a database so recurring words could remain consistent across the film.

That does not automatically mean every final-film sound is identical to the Peterson development notation. The finished voice passed through another sound-design process.

Do this before inventing anything

Production notation → finished film.

  1. Read the Rocky Test Sentences first. They're short and deliberately repetitive.
  2. Listen to several Peterson production audio tests so the three-line system is in your ear.
  3. Pick one or two clean Rocky exchanges in the finished film where the meaning is obvious from context.
  4. Compare musical contour: intervals, recurring voices, rhythm, chord shape and word boundaries.
  5. Don't worry if the absolute key differs. Relative movement may matter more.
  6. Record what matches, partly matches, or looks completely redesigned.
