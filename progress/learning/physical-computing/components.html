Component field guide.

A growing maker's toolbox. Learn what a part is for before reaching for a tutorial that happens to use it.

Studio deck
field guide · 01

Breadboard

▦
What is it?
A reusable board for building circuits without solder.
Good for
Prototypes, experiments, quick changes.
Watch for
Rows are internally connected in groups; the centre gap separates the two sides.
Use when
You need several components and want to change the circuit easily.
field guide · 02

Jumper wires

⌁
What are they?
Wires with plug ends for breadboards and header pins.
Types
Male–male, male–female, female–female.
Watch for
Colour is a convention, not electricity. A red wire is not automatically 5 V.
Use when
You need temporary connections between boards and components.
field guide · 03

LED

↗
What is it?
A light-emitting diode: current flows mainly one way.
Polarity
Long leg = anode (+), short leg / flat side = cathode (−).
Needs
A suitable current-limiting resistor.
Use when
You need a simple visible output or status indicator.
field guide · 04

Resistor

Ω
What is it?
A component that resists current flow.
Measured in
Ohms (Ω).
Watch for
Value matters. Colour bands or a multimeter tell you the resistance.
Use when
Limiting LED current, making pull-up/down networks, dividing voltage and much more.
field guide · 05

Pushbutton

●
What is it?
A momentary switch: it changes connection while pressed.
Input/output?
Input.
Watch for
Four-legged tactile buttons have internally paired legs.
Use when
A person needs to trigger an event.
field guide · 06

PIR motion sensor

PIR
What is it?
A passive infrared sensor that notices changes in infrared energy, often caused by warm bodies moving across its field of view.
Input/output?
Input.
Typical pins
VCC · OUT · GND. Check the labels on your exact module.
Use when
Motion lights, room detection, wildlife cameras, Lucky Cam.
A PIR detects movement, not identity. It cannot know that the warm moving thing is Lucky.
field guide · 07

Camera module

◉
What is it?
An image sensor and lens connected to the Pi through a camera interface.
Input/output?
Input: it captures image data.
Watch for
Connector and ribbon cable vary by Pi/camera model. Power off before attaching or removing the cable.
Use when
Photos, video, timelapse, machine vision and Cat Cam.
field guide · 08

Buzzer

♪
What is it?
A component that turns electrical signals into sound.
Input/output?
Output.
Watch for
Active and passive buzzers behave differently; check the part.
Use when
Alerts, feedback, timers and simple sound effects.
field guide · 09

Servo

↻
What is it?
A motor with position control.
Input/output?
Output.
Watch for
Servos can draw more current than a GPIO pin can supply. Power correctly; share ground where required.
Use when
You want something to point, open, tilt or move to a chosen angle.
field guide · 10

Ultrasonic distance sensor

)))
What is it?
Sends a sound pulse above human hearing and measures the echo time.
Input/output?
Both: trigger out, echo in.
Watch for
Many popular modules return a 5 V echo signal and require level shifting/division before a 3.3 V GPIO input.
Use when
Measuring distance without touching the object.
field guide · 11

Potentiometer

↔
What is it?
An adjustable resistor, usually controlled by a knob or slider.
Input/output?
Usually used as an analogue input.
Watch for
A standard Raspberry Pi has no built-in analogue input, so you need an ADC to read a changing analogue voltage.
Use when
You want a physical dial for speed, brightness, volume or a value.
